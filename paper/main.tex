\begin{abstract}
For a primitive character $\chi$ modulo $q$ let $M(\chi)$ be the largest partial sum of $\chi$ in modulus. We show that $M(\chi)\le c\,\eps^{-1}\sqrt q\log\log q$ for all but $O\bigl((\log q)^{C}q^{\eps}\bigr)$ primitive characters modulo $q$, uniformly for $0<\eps\le1$, with absolute constants. Consequently at most $q^{O(1/\tau)}(\log q)^{C}$ characters have $M(\chi)>\tau\sqrt q\log\log q$, and all but $\exp(O((\log\log q)^2))$ characters satisfy $M(\chi)\ll\sqrt q\log q/\log\log q$. The bound $|L'/L(1,\chi)|\ll\eps^{-1}(\log\log q+\log(2/\eps))$ holds outside a set of comparable size, and a zero-free half-plane $\Re s>\theta$ would give both bounds for every character with a constant of order $(1-\theta)^{-1}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $\chi$ be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo $q\ge3$ and
\[
  M(\chi)=\max_{x\ge1}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}\chi(n)\Bigr|.
\]
P\'olya and Vinogradov showed in 1918 that $M(\chi)\ll\sqrt q\log q$. Paley~\cite{Pal32} found infinitely many quadratic characters with $M(\chi)\gg\sqrt q\log\log q$, and Montgomery and Vaughan~\cite{MV77} proved that $M(\chi)\ll\sqrt q\log\log q$ for every $\chi$ if the generalised Riemann hypothesis holds. Without a hypothesis, the factor $\log q$ has been lowered only for special families: Granville and Soundararajan~\cite{GS07} saved a power of $\log q$ for characters of fixed odd order, and Goldmakher~\cite{Gol12} and Lamzouri and Mangerel~\cite{LM22} refined this. Bober, Goldmakher, Granville and Koukoulopoulos~\cite{BGGK18} determined, for prime $q$, the proportion of characters with $M(\chi)>\tau\sqrt q$ when $\tau\le\log\log q-C$; that proportion decays doubly exponentially in $\tau$.

This paper is about the characters on the far side of that range, those with $M(\chi)$ at least a large multiple of $\sqrt q\log\log q$. We show that there are very few of them, with a bound that is uniform in the multiple.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:A}
There are absolute constants $c_1,c_2,c_3>0$ such that for every $q\ge3$ and every $0<\eps\le1$,
\[
  \#\Bigl\{\chi\bmod q\ \text{primitive}:\ M(\chi)>\frac{c_1}{\eps}\sqrt q\log\log q\Bigr\}\le c_2(\log q)^{c_3}q^{\eps}.
\]
\end{theorem}

For a prime $q$ there are $q-2$ primitive characters, so the bound $\sqrt q\log\log q$ of Montgomery and Vaughan holds, with the constant $c_1/\eps$, for all but a proportion $q^{\eps-1+o(1)}$ of them. Read the other way, Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is a tail bound.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:tail}
With the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, for every $q\ge3$ and every $\tau\ge c_1$,
\[
  \#\bigl\{\chi\bmod q\ \text{primitive}:\ M(\chi)>\tau\sqrt q\log\log q\bigr\}\le c_2(\log q)^{c_3}q^{c_1/\tau}.
\]
In particular all but $\exp\bigl(O((\log\log q)^2)\bigr)$ primitive characters modulo $q$ satisfy $M(\chi)\ll\sqrt q\log q/\log\log q$, and all but $q^{O(1/\log\log q)}$ satisfy $M(\chi)\ll\sqrt q(\log\log q)^2$.
\end{corollary}

The corollary follows by taking $\eps=c_1/\tau$, and then $\tau=\log q/(\log\log q)^2$ and $\tau=c_1\log\log q$. For comparison, the frequency found in~\cite{BGGK18} is $\exp(-\exp(\tau'+O(1)))$ for $M(\chi)>\tau'\sqrt q$ with $\tau'\le\log\log q-C$; at the end of that range this is $q^{-c}$ for a constant $c$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} continues it, in the units $\sqrt q\log\log q$, to every larger threshold.\footnote{The two results use different information. The distribution in~\cite{BGGK18} comes from the structure of characters with large sums, which must pretend to be characters of small conductor and opposite parity. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} comes from the zeros of the $L$-functions of the twists $\chi\psi$ by characters $\psi$ of small modulus, through a zero-density estimate.}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} rests on a transfer principle: the argument of Montgomery and Vaughan needs the Riemann hypothesis only through a power saving in prime sums of the twists $\chi\psi$, and a zero-free rectangle of any fixed width supplies such a saving. The point of the present version is the dependence on the width.

\begin{theorem}[transfer]\label{thm:B}
There is an absolute constant $c_4$ with the following property. Let $\chi$ be a primitive character modulo $q\ge3$, let $1/2\le\theta<1$, put $R=\lceil\log q\rceil^{16}$, and suppose that for every $r\le R$ and every character $\psi$ modulo $r$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
  L(s,\chi\psi)\ne0\qquad\text{for }\Re s>\theta,\ |\Im s|\le q.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
  M(\chi)\le\frac{c_4}{1-\theta}\sqrt q\log\log q.
\]
\end{theorem}

The factor $(1-\theta)^{-1}$ is what makes Theorem~\ref{thm:A} uniform in $\eps$. It comes from the smooth numbers in the circle method: the major arcs need a cutoff $Y$ with $Y^{1-\theta}$ larger than a power of $\log q$, and the harmonic sum over integers free of prime factors above $Y$ is of size $\log Y\asymp(1-\theta)^{-1}\log\log q$. We do not know whether a smaller power of $(1-\theta)^{-1}$ is possible. The hypothesis~\eqref{eq:H} with $\theta=1/2$ is part of the generalised Riemann hypothesis, so Theorem~\ref{thm:B} contains the theorem of Montgomery and Vaughan. It also shows what a zero-free half-plane would give.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:half}
Let $1/2\le\theta<1$ and suppose that no Dirichlet $L$-function of a nonprincipal character vanishes in the half-plane $\Re s>\theta$. Then every primitive character $\chi$ modulo $q\ge3$ satisfies $M(\chi)\le c_4(1-\theta)^{-1}\sqrt q\log\log q$ and
\[
  \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)\Bigr|\le\frac{c_5}{1-\theta}\Bigl(\log\log q+\log\frac2{1-\theta}\Bigr),
\]
with $c_4$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:B} and $c_5$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:C} below.
\end{corollary}

No such half-plane is known for any $\theta<1$. The second bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:half} is the case of every character of the following analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} for the logarithmic derivative at $s=1$; under the generalised Riemann hypothesis $|L'/L(1,\chi)|\le(2+o(1))\log\log q$ for every nonprincipal $\chi$~\cite{IMS09}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:C}
There are absolute constants $c_5,c_6,c_7>0$ such that for every $q\ge3$ and every $0<\eps\le1$,
\[
  \#\Bigl\{\chi\bmod q\ \text{primitive}:\ \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)\Bigr|>\frac{c_5}{\eps}\Bigl(\log\log q+\log\frac2\eps\Bigr)\Bigr\}
  \le c_6\Bigl(\frac{\log q}{\eps}\Bigr)^{c_7}q^{\eps}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Here the zero-free rectangle that is needed has height of order $\log q/\eps$ instead of $q$, and the proof is a contour integral with a smoothed weight, in which the size of $L'/L$ next to the zeros is controlled by the partial fraction formula.

\section{Inputs}\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $p$ denotes a prime, $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function, $e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}$, $\|x\|$ is the distance from $x$ to the nearest integer, and $P(n)$ is the largest prime factor of $n\ge2$, with $P(1)=1$. For a Dirichlet character $\xi$ put
\[
  \psi(x,\xi)=\sum_{n\le x}\xi(n)\Lambda(n),\qquad\vartheta(x,\xi)=\sum_{p\le x}\xi(p)\log p .
\]
The letters $c,C$, with or without subscripts, denote positive absolute constants, and $A\ll B$ means $|A|\le CB$ with such a constant. A zero of an $L$-function always means a zero in the strip $0\le\Re s\le1$, written $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$.

\subsection{Character sums through Gauss sums}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polya}
Let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q\ge3$, $H=\lfloor q/2\rfloor$, and let $B\ge0$ be such that
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{m\le H}\frac{\bar\chi(m)e(m\beta)}m\Bigr|\le B\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in\R.
\]
Then $M(\chi)\le\sqrt q\,(B+2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau=\sum_{a\bmod q}\bar\chi(a)e(a/q)$. For primitive $\bar\chi$ one has $\sum_a\bar\chi(a)e(an/q)=\chi(n)\tau$ for every integer $n$ and $|\tau|=\sqrt q$~\cite[Chapter~9]{Dav00}. It suffices to bound $\sum_{n\le T}\chi(n)$ for integers $T\ge1$. Summing the first identity over $1\le n\le T$ and writing the residues as $-q/2<a\le q/2$,
\[
  \tau\sum_{n\le T}\chi(n)=\sum_{0<|a|\le q/2}\bar\chi(a)\bigl(e(aT/q)-1\bigr)\,g(a/q),\qquad g(\vartheta)=\frac{1}{1-e(-\vartheta)},
\]
since the geometric sum is $\sum_{n=1}^Te(n\vartheta)=e(\vartheta)\frac{e(T\vartheta)-1}{e(\vartheta)-1}=\frac{e(T\vartheta)-1}{1-e(-\vartheta)}$, and $a=0$ contributes nothing because $\bar\chi(0)=0$. With $x=2\pi\vartheta$, $0<|x|\le\pi$, one has $g(\vartheta)=\frac12+\frac i2\cot\frac x2$, so
\[
  g(\vartheta)=\frac1{2\pi i\vartheta}+r(\vartheta),\qquad r(\vartheta)=\frac12+\frac i2\Bigl(\cot\frac x2-\frac2x\Bigr).
\]
On $0<|y|\le\pi/2$ the odd function $\cot y-1/y$ has modulus at most $2/\pi$, its value at $|y|=\pi/2$, because it is decreasing on $(0,\pi/2]$ and tends to $0$ at $0$. Hence $|r(\vartheta)|\le(1/4+1/\pi^2)^{1/2}<0.6$, and the terms with $r$ contribute at most $2\cdot0.6\cdot q$. In the remaining terms put $a=m$ or $a=-m$ with $1\le m\le H$ (when $q$ is even the residue $q/2$ is not prime to $q$ and has $\bar\chi(q/2)=0$). They equal
\[
  \frac q{2\pi i}\sum_{m\le H}\frac{\bar\chi(m)}m\Bigl(e\Bigl(\frac{mT}q\Bigr)-1-\bar\chi(-1)\Bigl(e\Bigl(-\frac{mT}q\Bigr)-1\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
a combination with coefficients of modulus $1$ of four sums of the kind in the hypothesis, so they contribute at most $4qB/(2\pi)$. Dividing by $|\tau|=\sqrt q$ gives $|\sum_{n\le T}\chi(n)|\le\sqrt q\,(2B/\pi+1.2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twist}
Let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q$, let $1\le r<q$ and let $\psi$ be a character modulo $r$. Then $\chi\psi$, as a character modulo $qr$, is nonprincipal. If $\chi'$ is another primitive character modulo $q$ with $\chi'\psi=\chi\psi$, then $\chi'=\chi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $d=(q,r)$. Every $n$ prime to $q$ with $n\equiv1\pmod d$ has, by the Chinese remainder theorem, a representative $n'\equiv n\pmod q$ with $n'\equiv1\pmod r$, and $n'$ is prime to $qr$. If $\chi\psi$ were principal, then $\chi(n)=\chi(n')=\chi(n')\psi(n')=1$ for all such $n$, so $\chi$ would be trivial on the kernel of $(\Z/q\Z)^\times\to(\Z/d\Z)^\times$ and hence induced by a character modulo $d\le r<q$, which is impossible for a primitive character. If $\chi'\psi=\chi\psi$, then $\chi'$ and $\chi$ agree at every integer prime to $qr$; every residue class prime to $q$ contains such an integer, again by the Chinese remainder theorem, so $\chi'=\chi$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Zeros and the explicit formula}
We use the following facts for a primitive character $\xi$ modulo $k\ge3$; all are in~\cite{Dav00}, Chapters~16 and~19.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Z1)] For every real $t$ the number of zeros of $L(s,\xi)$ with $|\gamma-t|\le1$ is at most $c_8\log(k(|t|+2))$.
\item[(Z2)] For $-1\le\sigma\le2$ and $s=\sigma+it$,
\[
  \frac{L'}{L}(s,\xi)=\sum_{|\gamma-t|<1}\frac1{s-\rho}+O\bigl(\log(k(|t|+2))\bigr).
\]
\item[(Z3)] For $x\ge2$ and $2\le T\le x$,
\[
  \psi(x,\xi)=-\sum_{|\gamma|<T}\frac{x^\rho}\rho-(1-a)\log x-b(\xi)+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{x^{a-2m}}{2m-a}+O\Bigl(\frac{x\log^2(kx)}T+x^{1/4}\log x\Bigr),
\]
where $a\in\{0,1\}$ is given by $\xi(-1)=(-1)^a$ and $b(\xi)$ is a constant with
\[
  b(\xi)=-\sum_{|\gamma|<1}\frac1\rho+O(\log k).
\]
\end{enumerate}

The zero-density estimate we use is Montgomery's~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{Mon71}: for $k\ge1$, $T\ge2$ and $4/5\le\sigma\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zd}
  \sum_{\xi\bmod k}N(\sigma,T,\xi)\le c_9\,(kT)^{2(1-\sigma)/\sigma}(\log kT)^{14},
\end{equation}
where $N(\sigma,T,\xi)$ is the number of zeros of $L(s,\xi)$ with $\beta\ge\sigma$ and $|\gamma|\le T$, and the sum runs over all characters modulo $k$.\footnote{For an imprimitive $\xi$ the zeros in $0<\Re s\le1$ are those of the primitive character inducing it, since the Euler factors that differ vanish only on $\Re s=0$. Only the shape of~\eqref{eq:zd} matters below: the exponent of $kT$ is at most $\frac52(1-\sigma)$, and the logarithmic factor is a fixed power. A log-free estimate of Gallagher's type would remove the factor $(\log q)^{c_3}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} up to the contribution of the moduli $r\le R$.}

\subsection{Exponential sums with multiplicative coefficients}
The minor arcs are handled by an estimate of Montgomery and Vaughan~\cite[Corollary~1]{MV77}: there is an absolute constant $c_{10}$ such that, if $f$ is multiplicative with $|f(n)|\le1$, $a,r$ are coprime with $|\alpha-a/r|\le r^{-2}$, and $2\le R\le r\le N/R$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mv}
  \Bigl|\sum_{n\le N}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Bigr|\le c_{10}\Bigl(\frac N{\log N}+\frac{N(\log R)^{3/2}}{R^{1/2}}\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two elementary bounds}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth}
There is an absolute constant $c_{11}$ such that for $Y\ge2$ and every $H\ge1$
\[
  \sum_{\substack{n\le H\\P(n)\le Y}}\frac1n\le\prod_{p\le Y}\Bigl(1-\frac1p\Bigr)^{-1}\le c_{11}\log Y.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding each factor as a geometric series shows that the product is the sum of $1/n$ over all $n$ with $P(n)\le Y$; the second inequality is Mertens' theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gamma}
For $3/4\le u\le2$ and real $t$, $|\Gamma(u+it)|\le 15(1+t^2)^{-1}$. For $-1/4\le u\le2$ and $|t|\ge1$, $|\Gamma(u+it)|\le c_{12}|t|^{3/2}e^{-\pi|t|/2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The product formula gives $|\Gamma(u+it)|^2=\Gamma(u)^2\prod_{n\ge0}\bigl(1+t^2/(u+n)^2\bigr)^{-1}$. Keeping the factors $n=1,2$ and using $u+1\le3$, $u+2\le4$,
\[
  |\Gamma(u+it)|\le\Gamma(u)\Bigl(1+\frac{t^2}9\Bigr)^{-1/2}\Bigl(1+\frac{t^2}{16}\Bigr)^{-1/2}\le\Gamma(u)\Bigl(1+\frac{t^2}{12}\Bigr)^{-1}\le\frac{12\,\Gamma(u)}{12+t^2},
\]
by the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, and $\Gamma(u)\le\Gamma(3/4)<1.23$ on $[3/4,2]$; so the left side is at most $14.8/(12+t^2)\le15/(1+t^2)$. The second is Stirling's formula, uniform for $u$ in a compact interval and $|t|\ge1$~\cite[\S4.42]{Tit39}.
\end{proof}
\section{Prime sums from a zero-free rectangle}\label{sec:prime}

In this section $1/2\le\theta<1$ is fixed and $\xi$ is a nonprincipal character modulo $k\ge3$. The constant $c_{13}$ below does not depend on $\theta$; this is what keeps the final constant of order $(1-\theta)^{-1}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psi}
Let $x\ge2$ and suppose that $L(s,\xi)\ne0$ for $\Re s>\theta$, $|\Im s|\le x$. Then
\[
  |\psi(x,\xi)|\le c_{13}\,x^\theta\log^2(kx)\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\vartheta(x,\xi)|\le c_{13}\,x^\theta\log^2(kx).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\xi^*$ be the primitive character modulo $k^*\mid k$ that induces $\xi$; then $k^*\ge3$, since $\xi$ is nonprincipal and there is no primitive character modulo $2$. The two characters differ only at integers sharing a prime factor with $k$, so
\[
  |\psi(x,\xi)-\psi(x,\xi^*)|\le\sum_{p\mid k}\sum_{p^m\le x}\log p\le\omega(k)\log x\le\frac{\log k\log x}{\log2},
\]
and $L(s,\xi)$, $L(s,\xi^*)$ have the same zeros with $\Re s>0$. Apply (Z3) to $\xi^*$ with $T=x$. Every zero with $|\gamma|<x$ has $\beta\le\theta$ by hypothesis. The zeros with $|\gamma|<1$ are combined with $b(\xi^*)$:
\[
  -\sum_{|\gamma|<1}\frac{x^\rho}\rho-b(\xi^*)=-\sum_{|\gamma|<1}\frac{x^\rho-1}\rho+O(\log k),
\]
\[
  \Bigl|\frac{x^\rho-1}\rho\Bigr|=\Bigl|\int_0^{\log x}e^{\rho u}\,du\Bigr|\le x^\beta\log x\le x^\theta\log x,
\]
and by (Z1) there are at most $c_8\log(2k)$ of them. The zeros with $j\le|\gamma|<j+1$, $1\le j<x$, number at most $2c_8\log(k(j+3))$ and contribute at most $x^\theta/j$ each, so together at most $2c_8x^\theta\log(k(x+3))\sum_{j\le x}1/j\ll x^\theta\log^2(kx)$. The terms $(1-a)\log x$ and $\sum_mx^{a-2m}/(2m-a)\le\sum_mx^{1-2m}\le1$ are smaller, and the error term of (Z3) is $O(\log^2(kx)+x^{1/4}\log x)$. Since $\theta\ge1/2$, all of this is $O(x^\theta\log^2(kx))$. Finally $\psi(x,\xi)-\vartheta(x,\xi)$ is a sum over prime powers $p^m\le x$ with $m\ge2$, of modulus at most $\sum_{2\le m\le\log x/\log2}\vartheta(x^{1/m},\xi_0)\ll\sqrt x\log x$ by Chebyshev's bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pexp}
Let $2\le A'<B'\le x$, let $\gamma\in\R$, and suppose that $L(s,\xi)\ne0$ for $\Re s>\theta$, $|\Im s|\le x$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{A'<p\le B'}\xi(p)e(p\gamma)\Bigr|\le c_{13}\,\frac{(3+2\pi|\gamma|B')\,B'^{\theta}\log^2(kB')}{\log A'}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $V=\max_{2\le u\le B'}|\vartheta(u,\xi)|$, which is at most $c_{13}B'^\theta\log^2(kB')$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}. By partial summation,
\[
  \sum_{A'<p\le B'}\xi(p)e(p\gamma)=\Bigl[\vartheta(u,\xi)\frac{e(u\gamma)}{\log u}\Bigr]_{A'}^{B'}-\int_{A'}^{B'}\vartheta(u,\xi)\frac{d}{du}\Bigl(\frac{e(u\gamma)}{\log u}\Bigr)du .
\]
The derivative has modulus at most $2\pi|\gamma|/\log u+1/(u\log^2u)$, whose integral over $[A',B']$ is at most $(2\pi|\gamma|B'+1)/\log A'$. The boundary terms are at most $2V/\log A'$.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the major-arc estimate. Here and later $\chi$ is primitive modulo $q\ge3$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:major}
Let $1\le r<q$, $b\in\Z$, $\beta\in\R$, let $Y\ge\max(2,r)$ and let $t\le q$ be a positive integer. Suppose that $L(s,\chi\psi)\ne0$ for $\Re s>\theta$, $|\Im s|\le q$, for every character $\psi$ modulo $r$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\substack{n\le t\\P(n)>Y}}\chi(n)\,e\Bigl(n\Bigl(\frac br+\beta\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le c_{13}\,\frac{r\,(3+2\pi|\beta|t)\log^2(qrt)}{(1-\theta)\log Y}\cdot\frac t{Y^{1-\theta}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each $n\le t$ with $P(n)>Y$ factors uniquely as $n=pm$ with $p=P(n)$; then $p>Y$, $p\ge P(m)$ and $m\le t/p<t/Y$. Conversely, for $m<t/Y$ the admissible $p$ are the primes in $(A_m,t/m]$ with $A_m=\max(Y,P(m)-1)$. Since $p>Y\ge r$, $p$ is prime to $r$, and on the reduced residues modulo $r$
\[
  e\Bigl(\frac{umb}r\Bigr)=\sum_{\psi\bmod r}c_\psi(m)\psi(u),\qquad c_\psi(m)=\frac1{\varphi(r)}\sum_{\substack{u\bmod r\\(u,r)=1}}\bar\psi(u)e\Bigl(\frac{umb}r\Bigr),\quad|c_\psi(m)|\le1.
\]
Hence the sum to be estimated equals
\[
  \sum_{m<t/Y}\chi(m)\sum_{\psi\bmod r}c_\psi(m)\sum_{A_m<p\le t/m}(\chi\psi)(p)\,e(pm\beta).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:twist} each $\chi\psi$ is a nonprincipal character modulo $qr$, and by hypothesis it has no zero with $\Re s>\theta$, $|\Im s|\le q$. Lemma~\ref{lem:pexp} with $k=qr$, $x=q$, $\gamma=m\beta$, $A'=A_m\ge Y$ and $B'=t/m$ bounds the inner sum by $c_{13}(3+2\pi|\beta|t)(t/m)^\theta\log^2(qrt)/\log Y$. There are $\varphi(r)\le r$ characters $\psi$, and
\[
  \sum_{m<t/Y}m^{-\theta}\le\int_0^{t/Y}u^{-\theta}\,du=\frac{(t/Y)^{1-\theta}}{1-\theta}.
\]
Multiplying gives the bound, since $t^\theta(t/Y)^{1-\theta}=tY^{\theta-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The factor $(1-\theta)^{-1}$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:major} is harmless: it is cancelled in the next section by choosing $\log Y$ of size $(1-\theta)^{-1}\log\log q$. That choice is what produces the factor $(1-\theta)^{-1}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, through the smooth numbers below $Y$.
\end{remark}
\section{Proof of the transfer theorem}\label{sec:transfer}

Let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q\ge3$ and $1/2\le\theta<1$. Put
\[
  L=\log q,\qquad M=\lceil L\rceil,\qquad R=M^{16},\qquad A=\Bigl\lceil\frac{40}{1-\theta}\Bigr\rceil,\qquad Y=M^A .
\]
Let $L_0\ge e^{e}$ be an absolute constant, large enough for the finitely many numerical inequalities used below, among them $R<q$ and $72c_{13}M^{-6}(1+L)+c_{10}(16\log M)^{3/2}M^{-8}(1+L)\le1$ for $L\ge L_0$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:harm}
Suppose that $L\ge L_0$ and that~\eqref{eq:H} holds for every $r\le R$ and every character $\psi$ modulo $r$. Then for every integer $1\le H\le q$ and every $\beta\in\R$
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{m\le H}\frac{\chi(m)e(m\beta)}m\Bigr|\le\frac{c_{14}}{1-\theta}\log\log q .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split the sum according to $P(m)\le Y$ or $P(m)>Y$. The first part is at most $c_{11}\log Y$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth}. For the second let
\[
  \mathcal A(t)=\sum_{\substack{n\le t\\P(n)>Y}}\chi(n)e(n\beta),\qquad w(t)=\frac1t-\frac1{t+1}.
\]
Partial summation and $|\mathcal A(H)|\le H$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ps}
  \Bigl|\sum_{\substack{m\le H\\P(m)>Y}}\frac{\chi(m)e(m\beta)}m\Bigr|\le1+\sum_{1\le t<H}|\mathcal A(t)|\,w(t).
\end{equation}
Since $A\ge80$, $Y\ge R$, and $\mathcal A(t)=0$ for $t\le Y$. Fix an integer $t$ with $Y<t<H$ and put $Q=\lfloor t/R\rfloor\ge1$. By Dirichlet's approximation theorem there are coprime integers $a,r$ with $1\le r\le Q$ and $|\beta-a/r|\le1/(r(Q+1))$. As $Q+1>t/R$, this gives $|\beta-a/r|\,t\le R$.

\emph{Major arcs, $r\le R$.} Here $r\le R<q$ and $Y\ge\max(2,r)$, and the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:major} is~\eqref{eq:H} for this $r$. That lemma, with $b=a$ and $\beta-a/r$ in place of $\beta$, gives
\[
  |\mathcal A(t)|\le c_{13}\,\frac{R\,(3+2\pi R)\log^2(qRt)}{(1-\theta)\log Y}\cdot\frac t{Y^{1-\theta}} .
\]
Now $3+2\pi R\le8M^{16}$, $\log(qRt)\le2L+16\log M\le3M$, $Y^{1-\theta}=M^{A(1-\theta)}\ge M^{40}$ and $(1-\theta)\log Y=(1-\theta)A\log M\ge40\log M\ge1$. Hence $|\mathcal A(t)|\le72c_{13}\,tM^{-6}$.

\emph{Minor arcs, $r>R$.} Here $R<r\le Q\le t/R$ and $|\beta-a/r|\le1/(r(Q+1))\le r^{-2}$, so~\eqref{eq:mv} with $f=\chi$ and $N=t$ applies to the complete sum $\sum_{n\le t}\chi(n)e(n\beta)$. Removing the $\Psi(t,Y)=\#\{n\le t:P(n)\le Y\}$ terms with $P(n)\le Y$,
\[
  |\mathcal A(t)|\le c_{10}\Bigl(\frac t{\log t}+t\varrho\Bigr)+\Psi(t,Y),\qquad\varrho=\frac{(\log R)^{3/2}}{R^{1/2}}=\frac{(16\log M)^{3/2}}{M^8}.
\]

\emph{Summation over $t$.} Since $t\,w(t)\le1/t$, the two cases together give
\[
  \sum_{1\le t<H}|\mathcal A(t)|\,w(t)\le\bigl(72c_{13}M^{-6}+c_{10}\varrho\bigr)\sum_{t\le q}\frac1t+c_{10}\sum_{2\le t\le q}\frac1{t\log t}+\sum_{1\le t<H}\Psi(t,Y)\,w(t).
\]
The first term is at most $1$ by the choice of $L_0$, since $\sum_{t\le q}1/t\le1+L$. The second is at most $c_{10}(\log L+2)$. In the third, each $n\le H-1$ with $P(n)\le Y$ is counted by $\Psi(t,Y)$ for $n\le t<H$, so the term equals $\sum_{n<H,\,P(n)\le Y}(1/n-1/H)\le c_{11}\log Y$. With~\eqref{eq:ps} and the smooth part,
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{m\le H}\frac{\chi(m)e(m\beta)}m\Bigr|\le2c_{11}\log Y+2+c_{10}(\log L+2).
\]
Finally $\log Y=A\log M\le\bigl(\frac{40}{1-\theta}+1\bigr)\log(2L)\le\frac{82}{1-\theta}\log L$, because $L\ge e^e$. The proposition follows with $c_{14}=164c_{11}+3c_{10}+2$, using $\log L=\log\log q\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
If $L<L_0$, then every sum $\sum_{m\le H}\bar\chi(m)e(m\beta)/m$ with $H\le q$ has modulus at most $1+\log q<1+L_0$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:polya} gives $M(\chi)\le(3+L_0)\sqrt q\le\frac{3+L_0}{\log\log3}\sqrt q\log\log q$ without any hypothesis. Let $L\ge L_0$. The conjugate character $\bar\chi$ is primitive modulo $q$ and satisfies~\eqref{eq:H} for every $r\le R$ and $\psi\bmod r$, because the zeros of $L(s,\bar\chi\psi)$ are the complex conjugates of those of $L(s,\chi\bar\psi)$ and the rectangle is symmetric about the real axis. Proposition~\ref{prop:harm} for $\bar\chi$ with $H=\lfloor q/2\rfloor$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:polya} give
\[
  M(\chi)\le\sqrt q\Bigl(\frac{c_{14}}{1-\theta}\log\log q+2\Bigr)\le\frac{c_{14}+2}{1-\theta}\sqrt q\log\log q .
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} holds with $c_4=\max\bigl(c_{14}+2,(3+L_0)/\log\log3\bigr)$.
\end{proof}
\section{Counting the exceptional characters}\label{sec:count}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Let $0<\eps\le1$, put $\theta=1-\eps/8$, so that $7/8\le\theta<1$, and let $c_1=8c_4$ with $c_4$ the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Let $L=\log q$, $M=\lceil L\rceil$ and $R=M^{16}$. If $L<L_0$, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} gives $M(\chi)\le c_4\sqrt q\log\log q$ for every $\chi$ without any hypothesis, and there is nothing to count. Let $L\ge L_0$ and let $E_q$ be the set of primitive characters $\chi$ modulo $q$ for which~\eqref{eq:H} fails for some $r\le R$ and some character $\psi$ modulo $r$. Every primitive $\chi\notin E_q$ satisfies
\[
  M(\chi)\le\frac{c_4}{1-\theta}\sqrt q\log\log q=\frac{c_1}{\eps}\sqrt q\log\log q
\]
by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, so it suffices to bound $|E_q|$.

For $\chi\in E_q$ choose $r\le R$, $\psi\bmod r$ and a zero $\rho$ of $L(s,\chi\psi)$ with $\beta>\theta$ and $|\gamma|\le q$; it is counted by $N(\theta,q,\chi\psi)$. For a fixed pair $(r,\psi)$ the map $\chi\mapsto\chi\psi$ into the characters modulo $qr$ is injective by Lemma~\ref{lem:twist}, since $r\le R<q$. Hence, by~\eqref{eq:zd} with $k=qr$ and $T=q$,
\[
  |E_q|\le\sum_{r\le R}\ \sum_{\psi\bmod r}\ \sum_{\xi\bmod qr}N(\theta,q,\xi)
  \le c_9\sum_{r\le R}\varphi(r)\,(q^2r)^{2(1-\theta)/\theta}\bigl(\log q^2r\bigr)^{14}.
\]
Here $2(1-\theta)/\theta\le\frac{16}7(1-\theta)=\frac27\eps$, so $(q^2r)^{2(1-\theta)/\theta}\le q^{4\eps/7}R^{2/7}$. With $R\le(2L)^{16}$, $R^{2/7}\le(2L)^5$, $\sum_{r\le R}\varphi(r)\le R^2$ and $\log q^2R\le3L$,
\[
  |E_q|\le c_9\,3^{14}\,(2L)^{37}L^{14}\,q^{4\eps/7}\le c_2(\log q)^{51}q^{\eps},
\]
with $c_2=2^{37}3^{14}c_9$. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $c_3=51$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:tail}]
For $\tau\ge c_1$ apply Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $\eps=c_1/\tau\le1$. For the two particular cases take $\tau=\log q/(\log\log q)^2$, where $q^{c_1/\tau}=\exp(c_1(\log\log q)^2)$, and $\tau=c_1\log\log q$, where $q^{c_1/\tau}=q^{1/\log\log q}$; in both cases $(\log q)^{c_3}$ is of smaller order, and for the finitely many $q$ with $\tau<c_1$ the statements are trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The exponent $\eps$ enters only through the width $1-\theta$ of the zero-free rectangle, and the factor $(\log q)^{51}$ only through the number of twists and the logarithmic factor in~\eqref{eq:zd}. A zero-density estimate with exponent $c(1-\sigma)$ for any absolute $c$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $\theta=1-\eps/(4c)$, and the moduli $r\le R$ can be taken smaller, $R=(\log q)^{4}$ for instance, at the cost of a larger $L_0$, which lowers the power of $\log q$.
\end{remark}
\section{The logarithmic derivative at \texorpdfstring{$s=1$}{s=1}}\label{sec:logder}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:logder}
Let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q\ge3$, let $1/2\le\theta<1$ and $\delta=(1-\theta)/2$, put $L=\log q$ and
\[
  T_*=\frac{\max(1,\log L)}\delta+L+\log\frac1\delta+T_0+1,
\]
where $T_0$ is an absolute constant fixed in the proof. If $L(s,\chi)\ne0$ for $\Re s>\theta$, $|\Im s|\le T_*$, then
\[
  \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)\Bigr|\le\frac{c_{16}}{1-\theta}\Bigl(\log\log q+\log\frac2{1-\theta}\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $x$ be given by $\log x=\max(1,\log L)/\delta$, so that $x\ge e^4$ and $x^{-\delta}L\le1$, and let $T=T_*-1$. For $c>0$ and $y>0$ one has $e^{-y}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma(s)y^{-s}\,ds$, and since $\sum_n\chi(n)\Lambda(n)n^{-1-s}=-\frac{L'}{L}(1+s,\chi)$ converges absolutely for $\Re s=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin}
  \Sigma:=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\chi(n)\Lambda(n)}ne^{-n/x}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\Gamma(s)x^s\Bigl(-\frac{L'}{L}(1+s,\chi)\Bigr)ds.
\end{equation}
Replace the part $|\Im s|\le T$ of the line $\Re s=1$ by the path from $1-iT$ to $-\delta-iT$, then to $-\delta+iT$, then to $1+iT$ (Figure~\ref{fig:contour}). Between the two paths the integrand has a pole at $s=0$, from $\Gamma$, with residue $-\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)$, and no other pole: $\Gamma$ has no other pole with $\Re s>-1$, $L(s,\chi)$ is entire, and $L(1+s,\chi)$ has no zero with $\Re(1+s)\ge1-\delta=\theta+\delta$ and $|\Im s|\le T$. Hence
\[
  \frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)=-\Sigma+J_{\mathrm{left}}+J_{\mathrm{hor}}+J_{\mathrm{far}},
\]
the three integrals of $\frac1{2\pi i}\Gamma(s)x^s(-\frac{L'}{L}(1+s,\chi))$ over the vertical segment $\Re s=-\delta$, $|\Im s|\le T$, over the two horizontal segments, and over $\Re s=1$, $|\Im s|\ge T$.

\emph{The size of $L'/L$.} Let $1+s=\sigma+it$ with $\theta+\delta\le\sigma\le2$ and $|t|\le T$. The zeros in (Z2) have $|\gamma|<T+1=T_*$, hence $\beta\le\theta$ and $|1+s-\rho|\ge\sigma-\beta\ge\delta$; by (Z1) there are at most $c_8\log(q(|t|+2))$ of them, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ld}
  \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(1+s,\chi)\Bigr|\le\frac{C\log(q(|t|+2))}\delta .
\end{equation}

\emph{The left side.} Here $s=-\delta+it$, $|x^s|=x^{-\delta}$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, since $3/4\le1-\delta<1$,
\[
  |\Gamma(-\delta+it)|=\frac{|\Gamma(1-\delta+it)|}{|-\delta+it|}\le\frac{15}{(1+t^2)\max(\delta,|t|)} .
\]
With~\eqref{eq:ld} and $\log(q(|t|+2))\le L+\log(|t|+2)$,
\[
  |J_{\mathrm{left}}|\le\frac{Cx^{-\delta}}\delta\int_{\R}\frac{L+\log(|t|+2)}{(1+t^2)\max(\delta,|t|)}\,dt\le\frac{Cx^{-\delta}L}\delta\Bigl(1+\log\frac1\delta\Bigr)\le\frac C\delta\Bigl(1+\log\frac1\delta\Bigr),
\]
because the integral over $|t|\le\delta$ is at most $2\log(3q)\le4L$ and the integral over $|t|>\delta$ is at most $2\int_\delta^\infty(L+\log(t+2))\,\frac{dt}{t(1+t^2)}\ll L(1+\log(1/\delta))$.

\emph{The horizontal sides and the tail.} On the horizontal sides $s=u\pm iT$ with $-\delta\le u\le1$; there $|x^s|\le x$, $|\Gamma(s)|\le c_{12}T^{3/2}e^{-\pi T/2}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma} (as $T\ge1$ and $u\ge-1/4$), and~\eqref{eq:ld} applies. On the tail $|L'/L(2+it,\chi)|\le\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-2}<1$ and $|\Gamma(1+it)|\le c_{12}|t|^{3/2}e^{-\pi|t|/2}$. Hence
\[
  |J_{\mathrm{hor}}|+|J_{\mathrm{far}}|\le C\,\frac{x\,T^{5/2}\log(q(T+2))}{\delta}\,e^{-\pi T/2}\le C\,T^{5/2}(L+T)\,e^{-L-T/2}\le1,
\]
where we used $\pi T/2\ge T+T/2\ge\log x+L+\log(1/\delta)+T/2$, and $T\ge T_0$ with $T_0$ chosen so that $C\,T^{5/2}(1+T)e^{-T/2}\le1$ for $T\ge T_0$ (note $Le^{-L}\le1$).

\emph{Conclusion.} Since $|\chi(n)|\le1$, $|\Sigma|\le\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)/n+\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-k}\sum_{kx<n\le(k+1)x}\Lambda(n)/n\le\log x+c_{15}$ by Mertens' estimate $\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)/n=\log x+O(1)$ and Chebyshev's bound $\psi(y)\ll y$. Collecting,
\[
  \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)\Bigr|\le\frac{\max(1,\log L)}\delta+c_{15}+\frac C\delta\Bigl(1+\log\frac1\delta\Bigr)+1,
\]
and $1/\delta=2/(1-\theta)$, $\log(1/\delta)=\log(2/(1-\theta))\ge\log4$, $\max(1,\log L)\le\log\log q+\log(2/(1-\theta))$ give the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
Let $0<\eps\le1$, $\theta=1-\eps/5\ge4/5$ and $\delta=\eps/10$. A primitive $\chi$ for which $|L'/L(1,\chi)|$ exceeds the bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:logder} has a zero with $\beta>\theta$ and $|\gamma|\le T_*$. Now $T_*\le C\log q/\eps$, so by~\eqref{eq:zd} with $k=q$ and $T=T_*$ the number of such $\chi$ is at most
\[
  c_9\,(qT_*)^{2(1-\theta)/\theta}(\log qT_*)^{14}\le c_9\,q^{\eps/2}\Bigl(\frac{C\log q}\eps\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\log\frac{Cq\log q}\eps\Bigr)^{14}\le c_6\Bigl(\frac{\log q}\eps\Bigr)^{c_7}q^{\eps}.
\]
For the other characters Proposition~\ref{prop:logder} gives $|L'/L(1,\chi)|\le\frac{5c_{16}}\eps(\log\log q+\log\frac{10}\eps)\le\frac{c_5}\eps(\log\log q+\log\frac2\eps)$ with $c_5=20c_{16}$, since $\log(10/\eps)\le4\log(2/\eps)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:half}]
Let $\chi$ be primitive modulo $q$. If $\log q<L_0$, the bound for $M(\chi)$ holds unconditionally, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Otherwise $R<q$, every $\chi\psi$ with $r\le R$ is nonprincipal by Lemma~\ref{lem:twist}, and the hypothesis gives~\eqref{eq:H}; Theorem~\ref{thm:B} applies. The character $\chi$ itself is nonprincipal, so Proposition~\ref{prop:logder} applies as well, and $c_{16}\le c_5$.
\end{proof}
\section{Numerical data}\label{sec:num}

For every prime $5\le q<10^4$ and every nonprincipal character $\chi$ modulo $q$ we computed $M(\chi)$ exactly in the following sense. With $g$ the least primitive root modulo $q$ and $\operatorname{ind}n$ the index of $n$, the characters are $\chi_j(n)=e(j\operatorname{ind}n/(q-1))$ for $1\le j\le q-2$, and $\chi_j$ is odd exactly when $j$ is odd. Since $S(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\chi(n)$ is constant on each interval $[n,n+1)$ and periodic with $S(q)=0$, the maximum of $|S(x)|$ is the maximum of $|S(n)|$ over $1\le n\le q-1$, which the program computes in double precision; the accumulated rounding error is of order $q\cdot10^{-16}$, far below the resolution of the statements made here. This is $1227$ moduli and $5\,733\,937$ characters. The programs, written in C, are archived with the paper (see the declarations), and a second program, written independently in Julia, recomputes the largest $M(\chi)/\sqrt q$ for every one of the $1227$ moduli and agrees to six decimals.

The data show four things.

\emph{The maxima.} For $q>1000$ the largest odd value of $r(\chi)$ lies in $[0.714,0.842]$ and the largest even value in $[0.378,0.469]$. Only six characters in the whole range have $r(\chi)>1$, two of them have $r(\chi)>1.25$, none has $r(\chi)>1.5$, and all six belong to moduli below $12$, where $\log\log q<1$. The largest value of $M(\chi)/\sqrt q$ is $1.839$, at $q=8951$; the largest value over even characters is $1.003$.

\emph{Parity.} For each of the $1227$ moduli the largest $M(\chi)$ is attained by an odd character. Granville and Soundararajan proved, assuming the generalised Riemann hypothesis, that $M(\chi)\le(2C_\chi+o(1))\sqrt q\log\log q$ with
\[
  C_\chi=\frac{e^\gamma}{\pi}=0.5669\ldots\ (\chi\ \text{odd}),\qquad C_\chi=\frac{e^\gamma}{\sqrt3\,\pi}=0.3273\ldots\ (\chi\ \text{even}),
\]
and conjectured that $C_\chi$ is the true constant~\cite[Theorem~6 and Conjecture~1]{GS07}.\footnote{For even $\chi$ one has $S(q-1-x)=-S(x)$, so $S(x)$ is minus one half of the sum over $x<n<q-1-x$; the bound for even characters is the bound of~\cite{GS07} for sums over intervals, divided by two.} Both maxima in Figure~\ref{fig:max} lie between $C_\chi$ and $2C_\chi$, and the mean over $q>1000$ of the ratio of the even maximum to the odd maximum is $0.546$, a little below $1/\sqrt3=0.577$. At $q\approx10^4$ one has $\log\log q\approx2.22$, so terms of relative size $1/\log\log q$ are still large, and the data neither confirm nor contradict the value $C_\chi$.

\emph{The bulk.} The mean of $M(\chi)/\sqrt q$ is $0.70$ in every range and grows only in the third decimal, and every character in the range has $M(\chi)\ge0.4\sqrt q$. The distribution in Figure~\ref{fig:hist} has a sharp left edge, a mode in $[0.50,0.55)$, and a tail that decreases by a roughly constant factor per bin up to $1.5$ and much faster beyond it, which is consistent with the doubly exponential decay found in~\cite{BGGK18}.

\emph{The tail.} The proportion of characters with $r(\chi)>\tfrac12$ falls from $13.4\%$ for $q\le1000$ to $5.3\%$ for $9000<q<10^4$, and the proportion with $r(\chi)>\tfrac34$ falls from $0.43\%$ to $0.022\%$. Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} bounds the number of characters above $\tau\sqrt q\log\log q$ by $q^{c_1/\tau}$ up to a power of $\log q$; for $9000<q<10^4$ the data give about $500$ characters per modulus with $r(\chi)>\tfrac12$ and about two with $r(\chi)>\tfrac34$, so the true tail is far thinner than the bound, as one expects from a zero-density argument.

Three questions are suggested by the method.

\emph{Fewer exceptions.} The exceptional set of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is controlled by the number of characters $\chi$ such that some twist $\chi\psi$ with a modulus of $\psi$ at most $(\log q)^{16}$ has a zero with real part above $1-\eps/8$ and height at most $q$. In the zero-density estimate~\eqref{eq:zd} the twists have modulus $k=qr$ and height $T=q$, so the quantity $kT$ is about $q^2$; a smaller exponent in place of $2(1-\sigma)/\sigma$, or a version of Proposition~\ref{prop:harm} that needs the zero-free region only up to height $(\log q)^{A}$, would lower the exponent of $q$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} in proportion. The second change alone would halve it. In the present proof the height $q$ comes from the truncation $T=x$ of the explicit formula in Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}, applied to prime sums of length up to $q$.

\emph{The constant.} The proof gives $c_1$ in terms of the constants of the zero-density estimate~\eqref{eq:zd} and the Montgomery--Vaughan estimate~\eqref{eq:mv}, neither of which is explicit in the sources. With explicit versions of both, Theorem~\ref{thm:A} becomes a statement with numbers, which could be set against Table~\ref{tab:ranges}.

\emph{Even characters.} For every modulus in our range the maximum is odd, and the ratio of the even maximum to the odd maximum is close to $1/\sqrt3$. It would be interesting to prove, for a positive proportion of prime moduli and without hypothesis, that the largest $M(\chi)$ is attained by an odd character.

The large sieve and zero-free regions are used for related questions in~\cite{Bha26b,Bha26c}, and approximations connected with the Riemann hypothesis and with Ramanujan's $\tau$-function are studied in~\cite{Bha26a,Bha23}.

\section{The computations}\label{app:comp}
\emph{Character sums.} Two independent programs compute $M(\chi)$: a C program, which produced every number in Section~\ref{sec:num} and Appendices~\ref{app:small}--\ref{app:big}, and a Julia program written separately, which recomputes the largest $M(\chi)/\sqrt q$ for every prime $5\le q<10^4$ and agrees with the C output to six decimals for all $1227$ moduli. Both use the least primitive root, the table of indices and complex double precision; the partial sums are accumulated in order, so that the error after $q$ steps is of order $q$ times the machine precision.

\emph{Finite arithmetic.} A Lean~4 file, using the core library only, checks the integer facts used in the proofs: the exponent count $16\cdot2/7<5$ and $32+5+14=51$ of Section~\ref{sec:count}, the value $2^{37}3^{14}=657\,366\,253\,849\,018\,368$, the reduction of $2(1-\theta)/\theta\le\frac{16}7(1-\theta)$ to $\theta\ge7/8$, the bound $3+2\pi R\le8R$ for $R\ge2$ with $\pi<22/7$, the exponents $16+16+2+6=40$ and constants $8\cdot9=72$, $2\cdot82=164$ of Section~\ref{sec:transfer}, $5\cdot4=20$ of Section~\ref{sec:logder}, and the totals of Table~\ref{tab:ranges}. Every theorem is checked by the kernel; the file uses no axiom beyond the two standard ones of Lean's core, propositional extensionality and quotient soundness, and some of its theorems use none.

\emph{The Gamma bound.} The Julia program also evaluates $|\Gamma(u+it)|(1+t^2)/15$ on a grid of step $0.005$ in $u\in[3/4,2]$ and $0.01$ in $t\in[0,40]$; the largest value is $0.116$, well below the value $1$ allowed by Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, whose proof is in any case analytic.
